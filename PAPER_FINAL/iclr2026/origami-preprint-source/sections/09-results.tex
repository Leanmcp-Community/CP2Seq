\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\phantomsection\label{sec:results-headline}
The main ablations use the first ten fixed-seed instances from each of four groups,
with 360 planned sample--configuration pairs. Five protocol failures count as unsolved;
retries do not add denominator entries (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol-failures}). IDs follow
generation seeds, not model performance; selected samples match depth but have somewhat
fewer layers and creases (Appendix~\ref{app:sample-selection}). We report
the broader evaluation separately (Table~\ref{tab:headline}), since its samples and
budgets differ. All headline solves require CP match and $E_{\mathrm{id}}$; historical
$E_{\mathrm{plane}}$ counts are explicitly marked. Detailed analyses are in
Appendix~\ref{app:revision-details}.

\paragraph{Feedback without enumeration.}\label{sec:results-feedback-only}

underpowered, solved-instance sets differ, and repeated trials vary. We do not infer
additive effects or stable per-instance benefits.

\paragraph{Reasoning effort.}
GPT-6 Luna solves 6/30 some-layers sample--condition pairs at low effort versus 18/30
at high effort. Clustering by the ten instances gives a difference of 0.40
(95\% CI 0.23--0.53; exact sign-flip $p=0.008$; 10,000 resamples, seed 1). These are five-reference-fold
instances; the result does not establish performance on long sequences.

\paragraph{Matched search baselines.}
Table~\ref{tab:matched-baselines} uses the same 40 instances, legal candidate actions
and identity-aware success check. Random folding uses 100 independent 80-fold episodes
per instance; its per-episode success is 0.572 on easy and 0.959 on some-layers.
Model conditions reach at most 5/10 and 8/10 respectively in one episode each.
BFS solves 5/10 medium instances within $10^5$ expansions. Search establishes that
these targets are accessible; its larger total budget does not establish lower cost.
Random folding checks success after each accepted fold, whereas model episodes can
consume turns on rejections, observations and backtracking and must submit a solution.
The comparison therefore matches the legal action set and a nominal 80-step ceiling,
not the number of executed folds, stopping rule, inference compute or elapsed time.
Only hard is unsolved by every tested method. Its final stacks have a median of
404 layers across the full release, so failure cannot be attributed to depth alone.

\subsection{Failure analysis}
\label{sec:results-failures}
Cycling terminates 191 of 305 unsuccessful completed ablation episodes, but a stopping
condition is not a causal explanation. Trajectory categories are observable mechanical
rules (Appendix~\ref{app:matched-trajectories}), not internal capability diagnoses.
An automatic stop at the first CP plus $E_{\mathrm{id}}$ match raises solves only
from 25 to 27 in 143 saved episodes: most failures never reach the target.

The median ratio of distinct model states to random-folding states is 0.11--0.25
for text-only easy and some-layers conditions. Model episodes are often shorter
(20--45 turns on average versus 80 random folds), which accounts for part of this gap.
In 120 GPT-5.6 Luna L+A episodes, bounded search finds a repair within three removed
folds for 71/104 full restarts and 161/280 shorter backtracks. These observations do
not establish why a model restarts. After the first backtrack, 340/931 Luna accepted
folds revisit abandoned states versus 26/243 for Sonnet; such revisits need not be
errors unless the state has no legal continuation (Appendix~\ref{app:revision-details}).

\paragraph{Repeated trials and a second family.}
Five text-only Luna L+A repeats on 20 instances solve 7, 10, 6, 10 and 9 instances
(42/100 episodes), with coverage of 14/20 across five trials. Sonnet~5.5 solves
14/20 in one episode each (easy 5/10, some-layers 9/10); one timeout case counts as
unsolved, and its first four easy episodes used an earlier prompt order. These
small samples and differing repeat budgets do not establish a model ranking.
