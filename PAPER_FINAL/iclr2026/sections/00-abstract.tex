\begin{abstract}
Many tasks given to language-model agents require a long sequence of actions whose effects
compound, where each intermediate state must be tracked exactly and the result can be checked
only at the end. We introduce \textbf{CP2Seq}, a benchmark for long-horizon sequence planning with
exact verification, built on flat origami. Each of its 600 procedurally generated samples gives a
crease pattern and a target folded state, including the order of the layers; the model must
produce a sequence of simple folds that reaches the target from an unfolded square. A
deterministic folding engine executes each proposed fold or rejects it with a named reason, and a
completed sequence is accepted only if its final stack matches the target layer by layer,
including which region of the sheet each layer comes from, up to a rigid motion. Ground truth is
recorded during generation, and the released generator produces fresh instances at a chosen
reference depth. Origami supplies the state space; we make no claim about physical folding, which
would require thickness and contact. We evaluate GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Luna, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 on 20 instances, under increasing tool
assistance and compare them with breadth-first search and with random legal folding over the same
actions. On 40 ablation instances, no model condition solves a sample of 11 or more folds, and
higher reasoning effort raises some-layers solves from 6/30 to 18/30. The models fall short of
undirected search: within 80 folds, random legal folding solves 57\% of easy and 96\% of
some-layers episodes, against at most 5 of 10 easy and 8 of 10 some-layers instances in any model
condition, and breadth-first search solves 5 of 10 medium instances that no model episode
solves. Model episodes rarely pass through the target without stopping (2 of 143), and they reach
a tenth to a quarter of the distinct states that random folding reaches, consistent with a
failure of exploration rather than of the final step. Removing the rendered images does not lower solve rate, so the benchmark
measures reasoning over an exact symbolic state rather than visual perception. Single episodes per
instance vary substantially between runs, and the deepest group of the released all-layers corpus
grows to hundreds of layers; we report both and describe a layer-controlled regeneration.
\end{abstract}
