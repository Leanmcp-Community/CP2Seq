\begin{abstract}
Many agent tasks require long action sequences whose effects compound and whose result can be
checked only at the end. We introduce \textbf{CP2Seq}, a benchmark for long-horizon sequence planning
with exact verification, built on flat origami. Each of 600 procedurally generated samples gives a
crease pattern and a target folded state, including layer order; the model must find a sequence of
simple folds from an unfolded square. An engine executes or rejects each fold with a named reason,
and a sequence is accepted only if its final stack matches the target layer by layer, including
which region of the sheet each layer comes from. We make no claim about physical folding. We
evaluate GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Luna under increasing tool assistance, Claude Sonnet 5.5 on 20
instances, and breadth-first and random search over the same actions. Counts use exact matching,
one episode per instance unless stated. No model condition solves any of the 20 medium or hard
ablation instances (11 or more folds), and the models fall short of undirected search: within 80
folds, random legal folding solves 57\% of easy and 96\% of some-layers episodes, against at most
5 and 8 of 10 instances for any model condition. Failures reflect exploration rather than the
final step: stopping at the first match adds 2 solves to 25 in 143 logged episodes, models reach a
median tenth to a quarter of the states random folding reaches, and they restart about as often
when a short repair exists as when none does. Removing images does not lower solve rate, so the
task measures reasoning over an exact symbolic state. Results vary substantially between repeated
runs, and depth and layer count are confounded in the deepest group; we report both.
\end{abstract}
