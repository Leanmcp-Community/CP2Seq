\subsection{Assistance conditions}
\label{sec:ablations}

The reported conditions are \emph{basic tools} and \emph{basic tools with candidate filtering}.
Basic tools let the model add and remove folds, return to an earlier step or revision, inspect
the current state and images, and submit its sequence. Candidate filtering additionally provides
\texttt{list\_legal\_folds}. The basic-tools condition does not provide candidate enumeration or
the optional target-comparison tool. No-vision and verifier-only comparisons are not reported in
the current results.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Assistance configurations and current reporting status.}
\label{tab:ablations}
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.60\textwidth}}
\hline
Configuration & Status and interpretation \\
\hline
Candidate filtering & Results reported for \texttt{legal-folds}; available tools must be recorded per run. \\
Basic tools & Fold editing, backtracking, state inspection, images, and submission; no candidate enumeration. \\
No visual feedback & Planned controlled comparison; no results reported. \\
Verifier only & Planned controlled comparison; no results reported. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

A controlled comparison should hold the model, sample set, budgets, and other settings fixed while
changing the specified assistance. The saved aggregate does not establish this matching, so its
rates describe observed configurations rather than isolated causal effects of individual tools.

\subsubsection{Target-comparison feedback}
\label{sec:target-feedback}

The optional \texttt{compare\_to\_target} tool has three feedback levels, configured for the run
rather than selected by the model. These levels are separate from the assistance conditions above
and from the two final scoring criteria in Section~\ref{sec:scoring}. The tool reads the current
and target stacks without consulting the reference sequence or changing the state.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Implemented target-feedback levels. Their availability does not establish an experimental
benefit; comparisons require matched runs.}
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.72\textwidth}}
\hline
Level & Information returned \\
\hline
1: Counts & Current and target layer counts, their difference, and a count-based summary. \\
2: Diagnosis & Level 1 plus aligned layer matches, mismatched ranks, and a diagnosis of count,
shape, orientation, or ordering disagreement. \\
3: Corrections & Level 2 plus suggested corrections for up to twelve mismatched ranks, such as
orientation changes or a different target rank for a layer shape. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The richer levels supply more target-specific guidance. Their diagnoses are geometric feedback,
not proofs of a valid repair or substitutes for the final state comparator. In particular, a
matching layer count does not establish that the sequence is complete or correct.
