\section{Use of large language models}
\label{sec:llm-use}

ICLR 2026 requires that \textbf{any} use of a large language model be disclosed, on the Code of
Ethics principle that all contributions to the research must be acknowledged, and holds the authors
responsible for everything in the paper regardless of what assisted in producing it. The policy does
not treat a model used as a research instrument differently from one used for writing: both must be
disclosed. Disclosure is required \textbf{in the paper's text and in the submission form}, so filling
in this section is not sufficient on its own.

\textbf{Caveat.} Remember the submission form. It is a separate field and is easy to miss.

\paragraph{Large language models are part of the object of study.}
This is a benchmark for evaluating language models, so models appear in the work by construction.
Every number reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} is the output of a frontier multimodal model
driven through the harness described in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The models evaluated, their
versions and their settings are listed there. No model was trained or fine-tuned for this paper.

\paragraph{Code.}
The benchmark's implementation was written with AI assistance, principally Claude Code, used as a
coding assistant throughout. Every design decision, every experiment, and the direction of the work
were determined by the human authors, and all generated code was reviewed by them before it was
used. The authors are responsible for the correctness of the released artifacts. Two habits in the
project exist because of this reliance and are worth naming: the corpus carries two independently
written checks over every sample (Section~\ref{sec:cp2seq-verify}), and the state comparator ships
with a negative control that must reject a deliberately corrupted state
(Section~\ref{sec:scoring-validate}). The second of these caught a real defect, described in that
section, in which 23 of 600 corrupted states were initially accepted.

\paragraph{Literature search.}
The related work of Section~\ref{sec:related} was assembled primarily by hand, using Google Scholar
and alphaXiv. AI assistance was used partially, to locate candidate papers and to recover
bibliographic details. Every citation in this paper was verified against the arXiv or publisher
record by a human author; citations that could not be verified were removed rather than softened.

\paragraph{Writing.}
AI assistance was used in drafting and editing the manuscript. The argument, the claims, the
experimental design and the conclusions are the authors'. No text was included that an author had
not read and agreed with, and no result, citation or number in this paper was produced by a language
model without being checked against the artifact it describes.

\paragraph{Figures.}
Any figure marked \textbf{DRAFT IMAGE} is a placeholder generated with OpenAI
\texttt{gpt-image-2} and is used only as a compositional reference to be redrawn by hand. Generated
images are never used to depict data or geometry: crease patterns, folded states and result charts
are rendered from the corpus and from the run outputs. Draft figures will not appear in a submitted
version of this paper.
