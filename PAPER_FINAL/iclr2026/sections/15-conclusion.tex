\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CP2Seq pairs 600 generated instances and an open-ended generator with an environment that rules on
each fold and a scorer that accepts any valid sequence. The experiments support three observations. First, difficulty rises steeply with sequence
depth: models solve some easy and some-layers samples, but no ablation configuration solves a
medium or hard sample, and breadth-first search over the same actions solves 366/669 easy
attempts but only 1/40 medium and 0/16 hard, in line with a search cost that grows
exponentially with depth. Second, unsuccessful episodes end mostly under
the cycling stop rule, with the model returning to states it has already visited, rather than on illegal moves, which are rejected
without ending the episode. Third, assistance and reasoning effort
help within reach but do not move that reach: on matched samples, legal-fold enumeration, target
feedback and higher effort raise solve rates on short sequences, while medium and hard samples stay unsolved in every
condition. Because each ablation result rests on ten samples per cell, we report these as trends with
their uncertainty rather than as rankings of models.

Because the generator produces fresh instances with exact, programmatically checkable success,
CP2Seq can also serve as an environment for training rather than only testing, a use we have
not yet evaluated. Next steps are to extend the evaluation to other model families and to a
text-only condition that isolates the contribution of images; to add a random-legal baseline
and a symbolic solver such as \citet{creasy}; to compare models and search under
matched budgets; and to analyse trajectories decision by decision, so that failures can be
attributed to state tracking, target comparison or backtracking.
