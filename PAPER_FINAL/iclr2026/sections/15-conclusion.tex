\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CP2Seq asks a model to recover an executable folding sequence from a crease pattern and a target
folded state, and checks every step and the finished construction exactly. It provides 600
procedurally generated instances, a generator that can produce more at any depth, an interactive
environment that rules on each fold, and a replay-based scorer that accepts any valid sequence.

The experiments support three observations. First, difficulty rises steeply with sequence
depth: models solve some easy and some-layers samples, but no ablation configuration solves a
medium or hard sample, and breadth-first search over the same actions solves 366/669 easy
attempts but only 1/40 medium and 0/16 hard. Second, failure is a search failure: rejected
actions never end an episode, and most unsuccessful episodes in the broader evaluation end with
the model cycling through states it has already visited. Third, assistance and reasoning effort
help within reach but do not move that reach: legal-fold enumeration, target feedback and higher
effort raise solve rates on short sequences, while medium and hard samples stay unsolved in every
condition. Because each ablation result rests on ten samples per cell, we report these as trends with
their uncertainty rather than as rankings of models.

Because the generator produces fresh instances with exact, programmatically checkable success,
CP2Seq can also serve as an environment for training rather than only testing, a use we have
not yet evaluated. Next steps are to extend the evaluation to other model families and to a
text-only condition that isolates the contribution of images; to add a random-legal baseline
and a symbolic solver such as \citet{creasy}; to compare models and search under
matched budgets; to extend the some-layers corpus beyond six folds and admit pre-creasing; and
to analyse trajectories at the level of individual decisions, so that failures can be attributed
to state tracking, target comparison or backtracking.
