\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CP2Seq asks a model to recover the folding sequence between a crease pattern and a target folded
state, and checks the answer by executing it. Generating an instance costs one forward pass of the
engine while recovering a sequence has no known efficient method, which is what lets the corpus
grow without annotation and the verifier stay exact. On the easy group a deterministic
breadth-first search over the same action set solves 54.7\% of its attempts against 20.4\% for the
best-covered model, and no evaluated model attempt succeeds on the medium or hard groups. Every
recorded reasoning-effort setting is low, so these numbers bound what was measured rather than
what the models can do.

Two capabilities the benchmark is built to exercise are not yet trained against it: deciding when
to abandon a partial sequence, and deciding which candidate folds to discard unexamined. The
48.1\% of attempts that end in state cycling (Section~\ref{sec:results-failures}) is where both
are visible, and neither is addressed by a larger query budget. We report the gap and leave its
closure, whether by search-aware prompting, by tool design, or by training against the verifier,
to future work. The remaining assistance conditions, higher reasoning-effort runs, and matched
budgets across configurations are the measurements that would make those comparisons controlled.
