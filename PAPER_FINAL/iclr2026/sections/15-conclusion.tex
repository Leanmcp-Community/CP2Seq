\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CP2Seq pairs 600 generated instances and an open-ended generator with an environment that rules on
each fold and a scorer that accepts any valid sequence and checks the final stack exactly,
including layer order and sheet region. It poses long-horizon sequence planning with exact state
tracking in a setting where success is unambiguous and search baselines over the same actions are
available. The experiments support four observations, each on the uncapped release, under exact
matching ($E_{\mathrm{id}}$) and with one episode per instance unless stated.

First, the models solve some short sequences and no sequence of 11 or more folds, but this is not
a limit of the task: within 80 folds, random legal folding solves 57\% of easy and 96\% of
some-layers episodes (1,000 per group), and breadth-first search solves within $10^5$ expansions
5 of 10 medium instances that no model episode solves. Only the hard group is unsolved by every method, and in the all-layers tier it is
also the group whose stacks reach hundreds to thousands of layers, so we do not attribute that
failure to depth.

Second, failures are failures of exploration rather than of the last step. An automatic stop at
the first match would add 2 solves to 25 in 143 saved episodes; with legal enumeration,
unsuccessful episodes mostly never reach the target depth; and with exact coordinates models reach
a median tenth to a quarter of the distinct states that random folding reaches, revisiting many of them. When they restart,
whether a repair within three folds exists barely changes how often they do so (for GPT-5.6 Luna,
68\% of restarts versus 58\% of shorter backtracks had one), so the models do not separate a
recoverable near miss from a trap. Claude Sonnet~5.5 returns to abandoned states far less often
than GPT-5.6 Luna (11\% versus 37\% of later folds) and, in one episode per instance, solves
14 of 20 instances, as many as Luna covers in five repeats; one episode per instance on 20
instances is consistent with a stronger model but does not measure the difference.

Third, assistance and reasoning effort raise totals on short sequences. GPT-6 Luna's some-layers
solves rise from 6/30 to 18/30 with higher effort (three tool conditions, 10 instances each), and
for GPT-5.6 Luna at low effort with images, target feedback alone and
legal enumeration alone each raise the total from 2/40 to 5/40, and both together to 9/40. The
instances each condition solves overlap little and vary between runs, so these are differences in
totals, not evidence that the effects add.

Fourth, the models reason over the exact coordinates: removing rendered images does not lower
solve rate (GPT-5.6 Luna, 19/120 without images against 16/120 with), and images alone solve 1/40
with basic tools. CP2Seq measures planning over an exact symbolic state,
not visual perception. Because each ablation result rests on ten samples per cell, we report these
as trends with their uncertainty rather than as rankings of models.

Next steps are to re-evaluate on the layer-capped corpus, so that difficulty follows sequence
depth rather than stack size; to run several episodes per instance for every configuration, as done
here only for GPT-5.6 Luna L+A (pass@5 14/20 against 8.4 per repeat); to extend the evaluation to
other model families and sizes; and to probe model reasoning directly, so that failures can be
attributed to state tracking, target comparison or backtracking rather than located by observable
state alone. Because the generator produces fresh instances with exact, programmatically checkable
success, CP2Seq could also serve as a training environment, a use we have not evaluated.
