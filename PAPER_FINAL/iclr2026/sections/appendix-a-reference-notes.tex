\section{Reference notes}
\label{app:references}

The bibliography is grouped in \texttt{iclr2026\_conference.bib} by the role each work plays in
the argument rather than alphabetically, so that a co-author can see what is load-bearing. The
notes below record what is still outstanding.
Metadata corrections and source links are recorded in \texttt{REFERENCE\_AUDIT.md}
(25 September 2026). Source checks for the notes below are recorded there;
software provenance is recorded separately in \texttt{SOFTWARE\_PROVENANCE.md}.

\paragraph{Origami benchmarks and systems for language models.}
\citet{origamispace} is listed in the official NeurIPS 2025 program and is cited as a
conference paper; the absence of a venue on arXiv does not establish preprint-only status.
\citet{gamibench} has code and data released. \citet{foldingagent}
reconstructs folding processes from instructional video and is built on the PurelandFold dataset;
code at \url{https://github.com/maya-moriya/FoldingAgent}.
\citet{origamibench}, Section 2, reports 366 designs collected from Flat-Folder and
evaluates query efficiency, geometric similarity through intersection over union,
and semantic similarity through CLIP embeddings. Its query-efficiency definition
is the proportion of fold steps contributing to the final origami, not queries to solution.
\citet{corigami}, Section 3.8, uses a VLM for aesthetic and semantic evaluation;
Appendix B separately reports flat-foldability of generated crease patterns.

\paragraph{Complexity and theory of flat folding.}
\citet{bern-hayes} is the citation under the paper's central claim: geometry does not determine
layer order, and deciding overlap order is NP hard even given a valid mountain-valley assignment.
\citet{arkin-map} defines simple foldability; the action space comes from here.
\citet{akitaya-infinite} covers the all-layers model, which corresponds to sheet-metal bending.
\citet{akitaya-flatfolder} is the Flat-Folder paper, presented at OSME 2024 and
published in \emph{Origami8, Volume III} in 2026; deciding a global flat-folded state is NP
hard. See also \citet{mixed-orthogonal}, \citet{layer-algebra} and \citet{flat-folding-graphs}.
\citet{turing} is optional background. \citet{continuous} and its predecessor
\citet{reaching-folded} concern continuous folding motion.
The 2004 result concerns well-behaved folded states of simple polygonal paper;
the 2001 result treats rectangular paper. These continuous-motion results do not
establish reachability using our restricted sequence of simple folds.

\paragraph{The crease-pattern-to-sequence problem itself.}
\citet{akitaya-cp2seq} appeared in \emph{ACM SIGGRAPH 2013 Posters}; the Student
Research Competition is not its publication venue.
Section 2 describes reflection paths, graph rewriting, and a step sequence graph
whose paths yield folding sequences. The cited one-page poster does not report
the previously quoted frog-base runtime or node count.

\paragraph{Counting folded states.}
\citet{oeis-a000136} lists the foldings of a strip of stamps: 1, 2, 6, 16, 50, 144, 462, 1392,
4536, 14060, \ldots{} for $n=1,\ldots,10$. See also
\citet{lucas,koehler,lunnon,meanders}. Foldings and meanders are related enumeration
problems; do not identify them as the same object without specifying a correspondence.
\citet{meanders}, Section 1, attributes the 1891 statement of the problem to Lucas,
crediting Lemoine for suggesting it, and reports that no closed formula was known.
This is an attribution to Legendre's historical account, not a direct inspection
of Lucas's original page or a claim about present-day absence of a formula.

\paragraph{Spatial reasoning context.}
\citet{spatial-survey} surveys the area. \citet{vot} names the mental-imagery question this
benchmark can turn into a measurement.

\paragraph{Tools, formats and data.}
\citet{flatfolder} is the implementation that decides and enumerates flat-folded states, for the
method of \citet{akitaya-flatfolder}; together they are the source of the four constraint classes
cited in Section~\ref{sec:env-refusals}. \citet{fold-format} is the de facto
standard used by every artifact we ship. \citet{oripa} is a crease-pattern editor with folded-form
estimation; its bibliography entry refers to Mitani's 2007 journal article.
\citet{purelandfold} is not used as training or evaluation data here.
Its dataset card identifies it as FoldingAgent's ground-truth benchmark and reports
27 sequences and 337 annotated keyframes under CC-BY-4.0. The card requests citation
of the FoldingAgent paper when using the dataset.

\paragraph{Statistical physics of random flat-foldability.}
\citet{spin-model} represents layer-order constraints using spin interactions.
\citet{random-locally} studies random locally flat-foldable mountain--valley
assignments and proves that global flat-foldability is exponentially unlikely
on the square grid under its sampling distribution (Theorem 2). Neither result
establishes that multiple terminal states are typical in CP2Seq. For examples
and bounds on multiplicity, use \citet{akitaya-flatfolder}, without extrapolating
to the distribution of this corpus.

\paragraph{Deliberately not cited.}
Thick folding (Ku \& Demaine 2016), bar-and-hinge mechanics, MERLIN2, SWOMPS, Sim-FAST-PY, and the
creased-sheet mechanics literature all belong to the thickness and mechanics line of work. They are
outside the implemented zero-thickness, discrete-fold model. They should be
cited if discussing those physical effects; no runtime comparison is established here.
