\subsection{Tool and reasoning-effort ablations}
\label{sec:results-tiers}

\texttt{easy-}, \texttt{mid-} and \texttt{hard-0001}--\texttt{0010} from the all-layers corpus and
\texttt{layers-0001}--\texttt{0010} from the some-layers corpus, all of which have five reference folds. It compares GPT-5.6 Luna at low effort with

\paragraph{Tools.} Summing the Total column of Table~\ref{tab:luna-ablations} over the three model
settings gives, for the same 40 samples in each, 8/120 solves with B, 18/120 with L and 29/120
with A (two L and three A pairs are protocol failures counted as unsolved); the order B$<$L$<$A
holds in each setting. By group, easy gives 5, 5 and 10 of 30 and some-layers 3, 13 and 19 of 30;
medium and hard give none.

\paragraph{Reasoning effort.} Pooled over the three tool conditions, GPT-6 Luna solves 6/30
some-layers samples at low effort and 18/30 at high effort. Per condition the increases are
0/10 to 3/10, 1/10 to 7/10 and 5/10 to 8/10; with ten samples per cell only the second is
significant on its own (two-sided Fisher exact test, $p\approx0.02$; $p\approx0.21$ and $0.35$
for the others), and a 95\% Wilson interval spans roughly $\pm0.3$ (e.g.\ 0.11--0.60 for
3/10). Pairing outcomes by sample and condition, every discordant pair favours high effort
(12 to 0; exact McNemar test, $p<0.001$), so the effect is consistent even where single cells
are underpowered. It is measured on five-fold some-layers samples; on the easy group, which spans
four to ten folds, solves rise less, from 4/30 to 9/30.

\paragraph{Depth.} No Luna ablation solves a medium or hard sample, and the breadth-first
baseline solves 1/40 medium and 0/16 hard attempts (Appendix~\ref{app:bfs-analysis}), against
366/669 on the easy group. Within the easy group, the model solve rate falls from 32\% at four folds to
0--15\% at eight to ten (Table~\ref{tab:easy-depth}); beyond ten reference folds the benchmark is
currently out of reach for these models and nearly so for systematic search. This matches the
cost of search itself: breadth-first search time grows exponentially with depth, by a fitted
factor of 3.9, 4.6 and 12.4 per fold on the easy, medium and hard groups
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth-wall}, Appendix~\ref{app:bfs-analysis}). Depth is not isolated from other geometric differences
between groups.

\subsection{Failure analysis}
\label{sec:results-failures}

Rejected actions never end an episode, although they consume turns, so episodes end on
search-related stopping rules rather than on rule violations. In the earlier 447-attempt GPT-5.6 Luna snapshot, 167 episodes submitted a
sequence (63 of them correct), 215 stopped after cycling through previously visited states,
18 stopped on repetition and 47 exhausted the turn budget. Most unsuccessful episodes (215 of
384) therefore end with the model returning to states it has already seen instead of committing
to a new branch. The cycling rule stops an episode when the same fold is played from the
same position three times; we have not tested whether
looser limits would turn some of these stops into solves. The ablations show the same pattern: 191 of their 300 unsuccessful episodes end
in state cycling (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-terminations}).
