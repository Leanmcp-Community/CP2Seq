\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers below come from \texttt{workspace/RESULTS/results.md}, produced by
\texttt{python3 workspace/aggregate\_results.py} over every saved run. 1,211% fact:results.attempts
attempts across 47 runs and 256 distinct samples. Every attempt is counted once, and an attempt with
no recorded \texttt{solved} field is counted as unsolved.

\subsection{Headline}
\label{sec:results-headline}

\textbf{No language model solved a single sample beyond the easy stratum.} Across every model arm,
mid is 0 of 70 and hard is 0 of 27. On the easy stratum a deterministic breadth-first search over
exactly the action set the models are given solves 54.7\%, against 20.4\% for the best-covered model
at the same tier. \textbf{Blind search beats every model tested, by a factor of about 2.7 on the
only stratum where anything succeeds at all.}

\subsection{The baseline reading}
\label{sec:results-baseline}

\texttt{deterministic-bfs} solves 367 of 725 attempts overall. Per stratum: 366/669 easy, 1/40 mid,
0/16 hard. Median states expanded is 65 on easy, 1051.5 on mid, 532 on hard. 358% fact:results.bfs.timeouts
of its 725 attempts ended in timeout rather than exhaustion, so \textbf{50.6\% is a lower bound
under the baseline's time budget, not the ceiling of exhaustive search.}

This is the outcome Section 16.7 anticipated and the one \texttt{workspace/search\_baseline.mjs} was
written to detect. Its header records that \texttt{list\_legal\_folds} evaluates roughly 2,010
candidate actions per state and returns a mean of 3.51 that
are legal and stay inside the target crease pattern, over 73,750 enumerations. A branching factor
near 3.5 makes an easy sample of five folds a tree of a few hundred states, and the median of 65
states expanded on easy confirms it. \textbf{The easy stratum is therefore not measuring origami
reasoning.} It measures whether a model can avoid losing to breadth-first search on a tree its own
tool has already pruned roughly 570-fold, and every model tested loses.

We state this rather than report the models' easy-tier rate as an achievement. It is also the answer
to the ``the simulator does all the work'' objection of Section~7, and the answer
is uncomfortable: on the easy stratum the simulator does do most of the work. The benchmark's value
here is that it measures that rather than hiding it. On mid and hard the objection dissolves,
because search solves 1 of 56 and the models solve 0 of 97.

\subsection{What the tools contribute}
\label{sec:results-tiers}

The one within-model tier comparison the data supports is \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} on easy: 59/289
(20.4\%) with the filtering tool against 4/65 (6.2\%) with no tools, so filtering is worth roughly
$3.3\times$ on that stratum and nothing on mid or hard, where both tiers are zero.

\textbf{Caveat.} The no-vision and verifier-only tiers of Section~\ref{sec:ablations} were not run.
The four-tier ladder this paper describes is not yet instantiated; only its two endpoints exist.

\textbf{This is the most informative result in the section, and it is not a solve rate.} A majority
of attempts, 233 of 447 counting cycling and repetition together, end with the model revisiting a
state it has already produced rather than exhausting its budget. The model is not running out of
room to search; it is failing to notice it has been somewhere before. That is a specific,
diagnosable deficit in maintaining state across a multi-step spatial task, and it is the kind of
finding a benchmark with an executing verifier can produce and a multiple-choice benchmark cannot.
The baseline never cycles, because breadth-first search over a visited set cannot; it times out
instead, 358 times.

\subsection{Where difficulty lives}
\label{sec:results-difficulty}

Depth separates the arms completely. Easy is the only stratum where anything succeeds; mid yields a
single solve, by the baseline, at a median of 1051.5 states expanded against 65 on easy; hard yields
none from any arm. \texttt{[TO RUN: solve rate against coupling, to test whether the second
stratification axis of Section~\ref{sec:cp2seq-difficulty} predicts difficulty as intended. Only
depth is evidenced so far.]}

\subsection{Threats to these results}
\label{sec:results-threats}

\textbf{Every model attempt ran at low reasoning effort.} All 461 language-model attempts with a
recorded setting used \texttt{reasoning\_effort: low}; 25 have none recorded. This is the single
largest caveat on every claim in this section. ``Frontier models fail this task'' is not supported
by these runs. What is supported is that frontier models at low reasoning effort fail this task and
lose to breadth-first search where search works at all. Re-running the best-covered arm at high
effort is the most valuable remaining experiment, and until it exists the headline carries the
qualifier.

\textbf{Coverage is severely imbalanced.} 725 baseline attempts and 447 for \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna},
against between one and seven for every other model. No cross-model claim is made.

\textbf{24 attempts terminated in error with no model recorded} and are counted as unsolved. They
are reported as their own row rather than dropped, because dropping failed attempts inflates every
rate above them.

\textbf{Repeats are not yet reported as median and spread.} Section~\ref{sec:exp-protocol} promises
this; the aggregation counts attempts rather than grouping repeats per sample. The protocol requires
it before submission.
