Reviewer 1/2/3 below refer to the order in which the reviews were supplied, not OpenReview IDs. References use the section and table numbers in the accompanying anonymous revision. These are drafts for author review; they have not been posted.

GENERAL REVISION SUMMARY

Thank you for the detailed reviews. We revised both the verification and the interpretation of the experiments. The central changes are:
(1) Define identity-aware terminal equivalence, audit accepted solutions, and correct the primary ablation total from 55 to 50 solves out of 360 planned cases (§4.2.1, Table 2, Appendices A.6–A.7).
(2) Add matched-instance random-legal and BFS baselines with explicit budgets (§5.1, Table 4).
(3) Add feedback-only and input-modality controls (§5.1, Table 3; §5.2, Table 5), five repeated trials on 20 instances, and a 20-instance Sonnet evaluation (§5.3, Appendices D–E).
(4) Report instance-clustered reasoning-effort uncertainty (§5.1), clarify sample selection (Appendix B.15), and separate observable trajectory behavior from internal capability attribution (§5.3, Appendix B.14).
(5) Explain that terminal re-scoring does not correct historical feedback trajectories. Full historical auditing and corrected-feedback reruns remain incomplete (§6, Appendix B.13).
The revised main text ends within ten pages. Extended analyses remain in the appendices. We retain the original submission title in this revision and narrow the claims to planning over explicit geometric state, with operational verification under stated numerical tolerances.

RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 1

Thank you for identifying the discrepancy between original-sheet semantics and the implemented terminal comparator. We agree that this affects the validity of the original success counts, rather than being only a presentation issue.

1. Original-sheet identity and corrected success rates (§4.2.1; Appendices A.6–A.7; Table 2).
Yes: matching the crease pattern, current-plane layer shapes, orientations and stacking ranks does not guarantee the intended original-sheet correspondence. We now distinguish E_plane from E_id. E_id additionally requires each layer to contain the corresponding original-sheet region, allowing one global assignment-preserving symmetry of the input crease pattern. Independent relabelings at different layer ranks are not allowed. Missing identity data fail the check.
The audit of 536 previously accepted episodes found 514 that satisfy E_id. The 22 reversals comprise 18 model and four BFS solutions. Five reversals belong to the controlled ablations, changing the total from 55/360 to 50/360. Table 2 marks the affected cells. We added identity-sensitive regression cases involving congruent layers whose sheet regions are permuted; these are accepted by E_plane and rejected by E_id. The tests also check transformations and replayed reference states. We no longer imply that crease-pattern matching alone removes the ambiguity, or that numerical verification is a formal exact-arithmetic proof.

2. Selection and representativeness (Appendix B.15; §5).
Sample IDs follow generation order and consecutive seeds. The corpus manifest predates the ablations, so selecting the first ten IDs did not depend on model outcomes. The subset is close to the full groups in reference depth but has somewhat fewer layers and creases: for example, easy-group mean depth is 6.2 versus 6.8, while mean final layer count is 26.4 versus 33.0. Independence from performance does not establish representativeness in search difficulty. We explicitly acknowledge this limitation and do not generalize the 40-instance estimates to all 600 samples.

3. Feedback defects and effects beyond incorrect submissions (§6; Appendix B.13).
We agree that the effect cannot be bounded by the listed incorrect submissions. We distinguish the bottom-layer alignment bug from the identity omission, and distinguish the final scorer from the feedback presented during search. In 65 non-ablation episodes with retained per-turn feedback, all 1,025 comparison calls were replayed: one call differs under the corrected alignment logic, and two calls report a plane-only match that fails E_id. This audit is not the image-ablation set. Its per-turn logs were not retained, so we cannot claim that no other ablation trajectories were affected. The known affected cases have not been rerun with corrected feedback; the sign of the trajectory-level effect remains unknown. Historical feedback rows are therefore identified as such, rather than presented as corrected-feedback measurements.

4. Baselines and model coverage (§5.1, Table 4; §5.3).
We added random legal folding and BFS on the same 40 instances and legal-action sets. Random uses 100 episodes of 80 folds per instance; BFS reports expansion budgets of 80, 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000, with a reference-depth-plus-four limit and 600-second cap per instance. We clarify the differences in stopping, accepted-fold count and inference cost, rather than claiming equal compute. We also added 20 Sonnet instances and repeated Luna trials. These are bounded additions, not sufficient evidence for broad model-family generalization.

RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 2

Thank you for the specific suggestions. They led us to correct the terminal metric, add controls and narrow several claims. We address the questions separately below.

1. Identity equivalence, counterexamples and audit (§4.2.1; Appendices A.6–A.7; Table 2).
Yes, CP agreement does not imply original-sheet identity equivalence. The original comparator implemented E_plane, which can accept congruent layers at the correct ranks while the underlying sheet regions differ. We now explicitly define E_id: one global alignment must match layer geometry, orientation and rank, and one assignment-preserving symmetry of the input CP must match the original-sheet regions at every rank. Missing region data fail rather than fall back to E_plane.
Identity-sensitive tests include permuted source regions on congruent layers, transformations, and reference replays with cyclically shifted region assignments. The accepted-solution audit finds 514/536 successes under E_id, with 22 reversals (18 model, four BFS); the controlled total is corrected from 55/360 to 50/360. The 8,000 reference-depth random-rollout audit also finds eight CP-and-E_plane matches that fail E_id. These provide constructive counterexamples to the original implication. We do not claim a formal completeness proof of the numerical implementation: the tolerances and shared-engine validation limits are explicit.

2. Defective feedback and the two ablation episodes (§6; Appendix B.13).
We agree that checking final submissions does not bound the influence of erroneous intermediate feedback, and that fixing feedback need not improve performance. We distinguish two issues: the bottom-layer alignment defect could report matches with zero matching layers; the plane-only comparison could also miss sheet-identity differences.
Where logs survive, we audited every comparison, not only final submissions: 1,025 calls across 65 non-ablation episodes, with one alignment-logic divergence and two plane-only matches that fail E_id. The image-ablation per-turn logs are unavailable. We have not rerun the two known affected ablation episodes with corrected feedback, so we cannot report what those corrected trajectories would do. Their original outcomes remain explicitly identified as measurements under defective feedback. Terminal E_id re-scoring is reported separately and is not described as a corrected-feedback rerun.

3. Matched baselines and input controls (§5.1, Tables 3–4; §5.2, Table 5).
Random legal folding and BFS now use the same 40 instances and legal-action set. Random runs 100 episodes of 80 folds per instance, checking success after every fold; BFS uses identity-keyed states, reference depth plus four, a 600-second limit, and reported expansion budgets of 80 to 100,000. Random per-episode success is 0.572 on easy and 0.959 on some-layers; BFS solves 5/10 medium instances within 100,000 expansions.
These are matched-instance comparisons with explicit resource accounting, not equal-compute comparisons. Model turns can be spent on rejected proposals, observations and backtracking; random checks automatically and always has up to 80 legal folds. The replay stopping audit raises model solves from 25 to 27 across 143 logged episodes, bounding missed stops only on those retained trajectories.
Removing images while retaining exact numeric geometry gives 3/40, 5/40 and 11/40 solves for B, L and L+A, versus 2/40, 5/40 and 9/40 with images. With B, images alone give 1/40. These small samples do not prove that images never help; they provide no measured benefit here. We frame the task as planning over explicit geometry, not a validated visual-perception test.

4. Instance-clustered uncertainty and cycling (§5.1; §5.3; §6; Appendix B.14).
For GPT-6 Luna on the ten some-layers instances, higher effort increases solves from 6/30 to 18/30 across three tool conditions. Resampling instances with all their conditions together gives a difference of 0.40, a 95% percentile interval of approximately [0.23, 0.53] (10,000 resamples, seed 1), and an exact instance-level sign-flip p=0.0078. This describes the historical conditions and ten five-fold instances, not performance under corrected feedback or at greater depth. We avoid treating the 30 outcomes as independent instances.
Relaxing the cycling threshold requires new model continuations: already terminated trajectories cannot establish their counterfactual outcomes. Such reruns have not been completed, and the manuscript does not claim robustness to that change. We separate termination labels from observable error categories and internal causes. We also remove an intrinsic-depth-wall interpretation: depth covaries with layer count, and the hard-group BFS extrapolation rests on only two instances.

RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 3

Thank you for emphasizing model coverage and the limits of attribution from cycling. We agree that a Luna-only evaluation would not establish a general model capability result, and have revised both the empirical coverage and the claims.

1. Additional model coverage (§5.3; §6; Appendices D–E).
We added Claude Sonnet 5.5 under the text-only L+A setup on the same ten easy and ten some-layers instances. With identity-aware scoring it solves 5/10 easy and 9/10 some-layers instances (14/20), with a timeout case counted as unsolved. The first four easy episodes used an earlier prompt order, which we disclose rather than treating all execution details as identical.
For comparison, five Luna repeats on these 20 instances solve 7, 10, 6, 10 and 9 instances (42/100 episodes); five-trial coverage is 14/20. Sonnet's one-trial total matching this coverage is descriptive, not a statistically established model ranking or an equal-budget comparison. We have not completed the requested multi-scale open-weight evaluation. The revision therefore does not argue that Luna is sufficient or claim broad generalization across model families and sizes.

2. API access and reproducibility (§4.3; reproducibility statement; §6).
We agree that direct APIs can expose sampling settings more explicitly and improve control compared with CLI-mediated access. The current Luna runs use Codex CLI, and the added Sonnet runs use Claude Code CLI; they have not been rerun through public APIs. We document that limitation rather than implying direct-API reproducibility. Reporting an endpoint and explicit sampling settings would still not pin closed weights or guarantee deterministic backend execution. The revision specifies task instances, tool conditions, turn and timeout budgets, and the scope of retained artifacts; direct-API replication and open-weight experiments remain needed.

3. Quantitative failure analysis without internal-cause claims (§5.3; Appendix B.14; Appendices D–E).
We now distinguish a protocol termination label from an explanation. Cycling accounts for 191/305 unsuccessful completed ablation episodes under E_id, but does not by itself identify geometric misunderstanding, memory failure or poor backtracking.
We add observable analyses: replaying all accepted states in 143 logged episodes shows that oracle stopping adds only two solves (25 to 27); median distinct-state coverage is 0.11–0.25 of random-folding coverage in the text-only easy/some-layers conditions, with unequal episode lengths explicitly noted. Bounded repair search finds a repair within three removed folds for 71/104 Luna full restarts and 161/280 shorter backtracks. After the first backtrack, abandoned-state revisits account for 340/931 Luna accepted folds versus 26/243 Sonnet folds in the respective retained diagnostic episodes. These are conditional trajectory statistics, not independent task-level success estimates or proof of a particular reasoning deficit.
We do not have direct access to Sonnet's internal reasoning through the interface used, and no completed controlled intervention identifies whether memory, action semantics or geometric reasoning causes the observed behavior. Planned interventions and relaxed-cycling reruns are explicitly left as future work. The revised conclusions characterize measured behavior and evaluation limitations rather than inferring hidden cognitive causes.
