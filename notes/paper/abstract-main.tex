\begin{abstract}
% -- the setup: why the task, and why the obvious evaluation of it is broken -------------------
Recovering the fold sequence that produced a crease pattern is a multi-step spatial planning
problem with an unusual property: instances are trivial to generate and expensive to solve.
We evaluate tool-augmented vision-language models on this task against exhaustive search, on a
synthesized corpus whose (pattern, sequence) pairs are correct by construction.

% -- the framing measurement, already established -----------------------------------------------
Evaluation on existing collections is capped before a model is involved: of 366 published
patterns, 327 (89.3\%) are \emph{proven} to admit no simple-fold sequence, so a pooled
``percent solved'' has a ceiling of 10.7\% [measured]. We therefore report every dataset-level
number in strata --- proven foldable, proven not, and unknown --- and never pooled.

% -- the baseline the method is measured against, already established ---------------------------
Exhaustive search solves a five-fold pattern in a median of 4{,}002 simulated folds in the
all-layers action space and 99{,}016 in the some-layers space, rising to 56{,}250 and 754{,}552
at six folds, where 18 of 30 some-layers instances go unsolved within a million [measured].
This is the query budget a learned method has to beat, and it is reported over \emph{all}
attempts rather than only the solved ones, because conditioning on success hides the long tail
that is the whole difficulty.
